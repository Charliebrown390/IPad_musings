   - Geopolitical risk premiums on insurance/rerouting

2. **Upgrade quality bias within shipping credit:** Move to investment-grade operators (e.g., Maersk, CMA CGM bonds) with stronger balance sheets to absorb the pass-through gap. These have capacity to carry costs temporarily; weaker players do not.

#### For Multi-Asset/Inflation Strategy:

3. **Revise inflation pass-through assumptions downward.** The freight market is currently a **deflationary force for core inflation,** not inflationary:
   - Bunker costs are rising but not translating to rate hikes.
   - This suggests shippers and retailers will face *lower* logistics costs in H1 2025, providing a headwind to goods inflation.
   - **Policy implication:** If inflation expectations are anchored partly on shipping/logistics costs, this data suggests that anchor is weakening. Revise core inflation forecasts down by 10-20 bps for Q1 2025.

4. **Monitor Red Sea rerouting as a tactical hedging opportunity:** If geopolitical risk spikes further (Hormuz closure), rerouting premiums could spike suddenly. Consider:
   - Long positioning in reinsurance equities (they will price in higher maritime insurance claims).
   - Short positioning in consumer discretionary (extended transit times → inventory risk).
   - Long duration fixed income (flight-to-quality if supply-chain shock occurs).
