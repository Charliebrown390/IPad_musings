  2. Portfolio action: Maintain or slightly reduce duration on shipping lines' bonds. The 30/100 composite inflation score masks that operating leverage is negative—if rates fall 10% (as CN_MED shows), EBITDA compression will exceed volume decline due to fixed costs. Near-term (6-12 month) credit is defensible; medium-term (2-3 year) is at risk if demand doesn't recover by Q2 2025.

  3. Inflation Assumptions: The 80% weight on rate component means your inflation expectations should be anchored to demand normalization, not input costs. Current portfolio inflation hedges (long commodities, breakevens, inflation swaps) are over-weighted to supply-side narratives (Hormuz, oil costs, labour).

  4. Portfolio action: Trim long oil/commodity inflation hedges by 15-25%. The freight market is screaming demand weakness, which is deflationary. Reallocate to labour/wage inflation hedges (longer duration, as wage pass-through lags commodity cycles) or China growth/fiscal policy hedges (if Beijing stimulus emerges, it will drive freight recovery before it's visible in headline inflation).

  5. Reinsurance Cost Outlook: Shipping lines facing margin pressure will under-invest in fleet modernization and risk mitigation. This increases casualty and environmental liability tail risk, raising reinsurance costs in 2025-2026 cycles.

  6. Portfolio action: If holding reinsurer exposures (bonds, equity), expect hardening of marine insurance premiums even as freight rates soften—a rare divergence. This creates basis risk for correlation hedges.
