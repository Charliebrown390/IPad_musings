Rationale:

  1. Input costs are inflating (crude @ 99.4), but freight rates show zero pass-through (rate_component = 0.0) and are broadly cooling (5 of 8 mapped routes negative WoW).

  2. CN_NEUR stress (+58%) is a localized bright spot driven by geopolitical rerouting, not broad demand recovery. This concentration risk makes it vulnerable to supply normalization or further demand softening.

  3. Demand normalization post-holiday (negative momentum on US routes, stable elsewhere) combined with uncompensated cost inflation creates a Q1–Q2 2024 earnings deterioration scenario for leveraged ocean carriers.

  4. Geopolitical risk (72/100) is not reflected in sufficient rate premiums, suggesting either shipper contract fixity or carrier desperation. Either way, risk-adjusted returns to shipping credit are inadequate at current spreads.

Specific Actions:

  • For High-Yield Exposure: Reduce allocation to leveraged container shipping (CMA CGM, ONE, ZIM) bonds maturing 2025–2026. Target spreads of 450–500 bps on investment-grade carriers (Maersk) before re-entry.

Carriers will absorb 50–60% of input cost inflation due to demand weakness and contract rigidity. This dampens shipping-sector inflation contribution to broader portfolio inflation assumptions.

Timeframe: Monitor for rate stabilization or recovery at CN_NEUR levels (indicating demand re-acceleration) or further deterioration in US routes (demand collapse signal). Reset exposure triggers at these waypoints in 4–6 weeks.
