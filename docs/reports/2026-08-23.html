Key Finding: Asymmetric Pass-Through

The rate component dominance (76.4) paired with weak bunker fuel pressure (7.9) reveals an inverted cost structure:

  • Rates are already inflated relative to underlying fuel costs (bunker +7.9% over 4 weeks), suggesting freight rates have priced in inflationary expectations beyond current realised fuel costs. This is typical of markets anticipating future cost inputs (e.g., IMO 2025 regulations, port infrastructure costs).

  • Bunker fuel has NOT materially increased (+7.9% modest), yet freight pricing (rate component: 76.4) reflects higher-tier cost assumptions. This creates pass-through risk asymmetry: if crude stabilises or declines, rate reductions may lag, compressing carrier margins or forcing demand destruction to re-equilibrate.

  • Port congestion (68/100) is a non-commodity cost driver now embedded in rates. Congestion reduces effective capacity, allowing carriers to maintain pricing even as unit fuel costs decline. This is a structural cost not easily reversed.

Inflation Outlook for Investors:

  • Near-term (0-3 months): Inflationary pressure score will likely remain sticky at 35-45/100 because rate component is forward-looking and resistant to downward revision. Bunker costs provide limited relief.

  • Risk: Freight rate inflation may decouple from traditional CPI baskets, creating mismatch between portfolio hedges and realised shipping cost inflation.
