(3) GEOPOLITICAL & LABOUR ROUTE RISK

Geopolitical Risk: 72/100—Hormuz Strait as Primary Transmission Mechanism

The Iran Strait tightening is the dominant geopolitical risk. Evidence: - Shanghai-Persian Gulf routes spike (+3.3% to +4.7%): Shippers are repricing risk into rates for Gulf-bound cargoes, suggesting either (a) reduced confidence in unimpeded passage, or (b) anticipation of piracy premiums / insurance surcharges. This is one of the few regions showing demand-driven rate appreciation, indicating shippers are willing to pay more to secure Gulf access.

  • Asia-Europe reallocation (+6.9% spike on Shanghai-Europe) concurrent with Hormuz tensions: This is classic route substitution. Shippers unable or unwilling to commit to Hormuz risk are rerouting via Suez to Europe instead, creating artificial demand and rate pressure on long-haul Europe routes. This is demand elasticity being driven by geopolitical, not macro, factors.

  • Shanghai-Rotterdam COOLING (-4.3%): The paradox here is instructive. If Hormuz tensions were creating universal rerouting to long-haul alternatives, Rotterdam rates should spike (Suez-Europe route). Instead, they cool, suggesting: (i) Rotterdam-bound cargo may transit via existing Suez capacity without rate pressure, or (ii) Rotterdam demand has softened independent of routing, indicating European import weakness is real, not just rerouting noise.

This indicates latent concern that current labour/insurance costs will rise materially if Hormuz tensions escalate. Labour not currently spiking, but optionality is being priced.

Implication: Congestion risk is being monetized selectively by ports with structural constraints (Suez alternatives, Mediterranean choke points) but not by ports with capacity overhang (LA, Long Beach).
