Recommendation: Recalibrate Inflation Assumptions & Reduce Shipping Sector Credit Exposure

Inflation Assumptions: The composite score of 44/100 masks a critical divergence: the 86.6/100 rate component shows carriers have already front-loaded inflation pricing, while demand indices show erosion. This means:

  • CPI expectations must pivot downward for shipping-dependent sectors (e.g., consumer discretionary importers, retail CPG firms). If freight rates deflate faster than input costs (crude component at 51.9/100 remains sticky), shipper margins compress, reducing pricing power downstream.
  • Inflation hedging via shipping-linked bonds is no longer valid. Carriers' past pricing power is exhausted; future inflation will not be transmitted to freight rates in a demand-soft environment.

Credit Exposure Recommendation: - Downgrade outlook on mid-tier carrier bonds (BBB/Ba ratings). The -14.7% CN_OCE collapse + -19.9% FBX14 deterioration signals demand destruction faster than cost relief. Carriers with high fixed-cost structures (fuel, labour, vessel debt) face margin compression. - Avoid new long-duration exposure to shipping lines with leverage > 3.0x net debt/EBITDA. Refinancing risk rises as rates are pressured. - Preferred positioning: Invest in asset-light logistics platforms (3PL, freight forwarding) that can flex cost structures with demand, rather than capital-intensive carriers locked into vessel contracts.

Reinsurance Cost Outlook: Geopolitical risk (72/100) + port congestion (68/100) will drive marine insurance premium expansion 10-15% in 2024. Reinsurance cost inflation is not being passed through to end-user freight rates—this margin leak reduces shipping company profitability and should be reflected in equity risk premiums and credit spreads (+75-150 bps for BB-rated carriers).

Bottom Line: Freight markets are in demand-led contraction with geopolitical tail risks. Rate inflation has already peaked; cost deflation is asymmetric. Reduce shipping credit exposure and recalibrate portfolio inflation assumptions downward for 2H2024.
